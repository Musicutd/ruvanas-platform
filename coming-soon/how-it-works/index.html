How Ruvanas works

These workflows illustrate planned platform capabilities. Ruvanas is in private preview; no public registration or customer service is offered from this page. Access is by administrator-issued code only.

RETAIL RADIO

Run consistent radio across every location

Retail Radio connects your approved music, promotions, schedules and physical players. You prepare the experience in the dashboard; each enrolled player receives the correct programme for its location and zone.

THE RESULTA controlled in-store service that keeps playing, follows local schedules and records delivery evidence.
1. Confirm the account and serviceKnow who can make changes and which plan allowances apply.

Your organisation is the security and billing boundary. Owners and managers control setup; other team roles see only the tools allowed to them. Account & plan shows the active Retail plan, location allowance, player allowance and optional services.

What the workflow involves

  1. Open Account & plan and confirm Retail Radio is active.
  2. Open Organisation & team and give each colleague the smallest suitable role.
  3. Check Notification centre for anything that blocks setup or playback.
2. Create locations and listening zonesDescribe where audio will play before connecting devices.

A location represents a physical shop or venue. A zone represents a listening area inside it, such as the shop floor, café or entrance. Schedules and players use these records so content cannot accidentally cross into another location.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create the physical location with its local timezone and opening hours.
  2. Add one or more clearly named zones inside the location.
  3. Use location groups only when several shops should share the same controlled setup.
3. Prepare music and audio safelyBuild a rights-cleared library without exposing storage details.

Your Media library holds organisation-owned music, promotions and voice material. Every music track needs clear rights, permitted territories and product uses. Ruvanas catalogue music remains subject to the genre access included in the current plan.

What the workflow involves

  1. Upload audio and enter accurate title, artist and rights information.
  2. Select Retail Radio only when the licence permits retail use.
  3. Wait for the item to become approved or ready before using it in programming.
4. Build programming and AutoDJChoose what plays, when it plays and what fills every gap.

Programming combines Music Modes, schedules and AutoDJ. Music Modes define an approved rotation. Weekly schedules decide when each mode applies. Continuous AutoDJ protects unscheduled time, while timed playlist generation prepares a finite, reviewable sequence for a chosen date and window.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create or select an approved Music Mode for the required mood.
  2. Add weekly schedule blocks in the location timezone.
  3. Configure Continuous AutoDJ as the gap-filling default and optional backup.
  4. Use Timed playlist for a fixed, versioned programme in a particular window.
  5. Preview the result before publishing it.
5. Add promotions without breaking the music flowSchedule approved messages with dates, times and delivery rules.

Promotions are controlled insertions, not replacements for the underlying music schedule. Each promotion carries its own campaign window, destination and approval status. The playout resolver inserts only currently eligible material.

What the workflow involves

  1. Prepare or upload the promotional audio.
  2. Choose the correct Retail destination and campaign dates.
  3. Review the delivery preview and publish only approved material.
  4. Use delivery reports to confirm completed plays.
6. Enrol and place each playerConnect the device that turns the plan into audio in the shop.

A player belongs to one organisation and one location zone. The one-time enrolment code establishes that relationship. Once online, the player asks the shared resolver for the correct programme and reports operational evidence back to Ruvanas.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create the player for the exact location and zone.
  2. Open the player page on the listening device and enter the one-time code.
  3. Keep one active player session per intended audio device.
  4. Check that the player reports online before leaving the location.
7. Monitor service and delivery evidenceSee what is online, what played and what needs attention.

Service insights shows operational trends, while Live streams shows current connections. Delivery reports use verified proof-of-play records rather than assuming that a scheduled item played. Notifications surface issues that need an owner or manager.

What the workflow involves

  1. Check Live streams for active device sessions.
  2. Review Service insights for interruptions or unusual trends.
  3. Open Delivery reports when a campaign needs evidence.
  4. Follow Notification centre actions instead of repeatedly changing a healthy schedule.
8. Add optional visual and production servicesUse Digital Signage, Retail Media or Studio only when included.

Optional services reuse the same organisation boundary. Digital Signage manages screens, visual playlists and takeovers. Retail Media coordinates governed campaigns. Ruvanas Studio provides professional production requests without giving production staff uncontrolled access to your radio setup.

What the workflow involves

  1. Confirm the optional service appears in Account & plan.
  2. Open its dedicated workspace rather than mixing visual and audio devices.
  3. Keep approvals and delivery evidence with the related campaign or production order.

SCHOOL RADIO

Create radio with safeguarding built in

School Radio is a staff-managed workspace for supervised programmes, learning and controlled publishing. Safeguarding, consent and staff responsibility come before student access or public release.

THE RESULTA private-by-default environment where staff remain responsible for every student workflow and publication decision.
1. Complete safeguarding readiness firstRecord the school rules before inviting students or publishing.

The safeguarding pack defines territory, age and consent expectations, the responsible privacy contact, moderation arrangements and retention. Completing it does not automatically enable students, messaging or public publishing; it creates the controlled policy boundary those later actions must respect.

What the workflow involves

  1. Open the School production suite and start in Overview.
  2. Complete territory, age, consent, privacy and retention information.
  3. Name the responsible safeguarding and operational contacts.
  4. Submit the pack for the required staff approval.
2. Assign staff responsibility and student accessKeep supervision explicit and access time-bounded.

Staff roles and student access are separate. An authorised staff member supervises the programme and remains accountable for review. Student invitations are limited to the school workspace and do not create general subscriber or administration access.

What the workflow involves

  1. Add staff through Organisation & team with the correct role.
  2. Assign the staff supervisor before creating a student-led programme.
  3. Invite students only through the guarded student-access workflow.
  4. Revoke access promptly when participation ends.
3. Prepare school locations and private listeningConnect school audio without making the workspace public.

Physical School Radio uses locations, zones and enrolled players in the same protected delivery core as Retail Radio, but school policy remains stricter. A classroom, studio or common area should have its own clearly identified zone and device assignment.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create the school location and local timezone.
  2. Add only the listening zones the school intends to operate.
  3. Enrol a player for the exact zone and confirm it is online.
  4. Keep public publishing disabled unless the separate approval path is complete.
4. Record and edit in the production StudioCreate audio non-destructively from recording to mastered review.

The School Studio provides guided recording, waveform editing, voice cleanup, effects, mastering and multitrack tools. Original recordings remain protected. Edits create recoverable versions, and rendered previews remain subject to staff review.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create a project and record or upload a protected take.
  2. Trim, fade and save waveform edits as a new version.
  3. Use voice cleanup and compare Before and After previews.
  4. Apply a delivery preset and confirm mastering quality.
  5. Build and render the multitrack project, then send it for staff review.
5. Build programmes and editorial contentTurn supervised ideas into structured shows and stories.

Programmes group episodes, production responsibility and release decisions. Show Builder, news and editorial tools keep scripts, fact-checking, audio and approvals connected. Saving a draft never means it is approved or public.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create the programme and assign its staff supervisor.
  2. Prepare the episode outline, script or newsroom assignment.
  3. Attach facts, sources and production material where required.
  4. Complete the staff editorial review before requesting publication.
6. Review, approve and publish deliberatelySeparate production completion from permission to release.

A finished recording can still be unsuitable for release. School publication checks safeguarding readiness, staff authority, editorial state, rights and the chosen destination. Private playback, a podcast and a public page are different destinations with different consequences.

What the workflow involves

  1. Confirm the final audio and metadata are correct.
  2. Choose the intended private or approved public destination.
  3. Complete the staff review and publication checklist.
  4. Publish once, then verify the destination rather than creating duplicates.
  5. Withdraw the release immediately if safeguarding or rights change.
7. Use learning and school exchange safelyKeep assessment and collaboration inside guarded boundaries.

Learning tools connect submissions, assessments and staff feedback without exposing general account data. School Exchange shares only approved material between explicitly participating schools. Neither feature turns the school workspace into open social messaging.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create the learning activity or approved exchange item.
  2. Limit participation to the intended school members.
  3. Review submissions and release staff feedback deliberately.
  4. Remove or withdraw shared material when its approval no longer applies.
8. Monitor privacy, retention and supportReview evidence without exposing student information.

School operations preserve audit and moderation evidence under the stated retention rules. Notifications identify required action without placing private student content in general dashboards. Owners and authorised staff should use Support requests when the safe next step is unclear.

What the workflow involves

  1. Review school readiness and outstanding moderation work.
  2. Follow the approved retention and withdrawal process.
  3. Keep student information out of screenshots and ordinary support notes.
  4. Send a secure support request when staff cannot resolve the issue.

ONLINE RADIO

Operate a complete online radio station

Online Radio brings station identity, music programming, live operation, public listening, podcasts, advertising and reporting into one governed service. The public player receives only material that the shared resolver considers active and eligible.

THE RESULTA professional online station with controlled scheduling, resilient playout, public listening and attributable delivery evidence.
1. Create the station and confirm its identitySet the foundation before publishing a player or website.

The station record holds the listener-facing name, slug, stream configuration and channel relationship. It belongs to the signed-in organisation and stays separate from Retail and School products even when the same team operates them.

What the workflow involves

  1. Open Online Radio and create the first station if prompted.
  2. Confirm the station name, public identity and required stream settings.
  3. Complete the setup checks before sharing listener links.
  4. Use Station networks only for explicit cross-organisation relationships.
2. Build the rights-cleared music libraryPrepare content the scheduler and resolver are allowed to use.

Organisation uploads remain separate from the Ruvanas catalogues. Track eligibility considers approval state, rights dates, territory, explicit-content rules and Online Radio product use. Locked catalogue genres cannot be selected by changing a browser request.

What the workflow involves

  1. Upload owned or licensed material in Media library.
  2. Enter accurate rights, territory and Online Radio usage information.
  3. Choose genres that reflect the recording and current plan access.
  4. Wait for approval before adding a track to live programming.
3. Plan music with modes, schedules and AutoDJCreate a deliberate sound while protecting every unscheduled minute.

Music Modes define rotations. The Advanced Scheduler and Radio Clocks place programmes and exact-time events. Continuous AutoDJ fills gaps using approved sources and a backup policy. Timed playlists generate a finite, reviewable sequence with frozen versions for a specific local window.

What the workflow involves

  1. Prepare Music Modes for the station formats or dayparts.
  2. Build the weekly schedule in the station timezone.
  3. Configure Continuous AutoDJ and keep a distinct backup where available.
  4. Generate a Timed playlist for special windows, review its order and publish the approved version.
  5. Use schedule preview to identify gaps or conflicts.
4. Operate live and recorded programmingUse live sources without sacrificing scheduled fallback.

External Live, Browser Live Studio, DJ access and voice tracking are controlled inputs to the same playout decision. A live source must be healthy and authorised. When unavailable, the resolver returns to eligible scheduled or AutoDJ material instead of claiming that silence is live content.

What the workflow involves

  1. Prepare the live source and verify its health before activation.
  2. Grant presenters only the station, channel and time window they need.
  3. Keep scheduled or AutoDJ material ready as fallback.
  4. End the live session cleanly and review its operational evidence.
5. Publish the public player and station websiteGive listeners a branded, capacity-controlled destination.

The Public player is the approved web listening surface and can provide an embed. The station website adds now-playing, podcasts, installable mobile behaviour and optional verified-domain routing. Listener sessions remain capacity controlled and privacy aware.

What the workflow involves

  1. Complete station setup and verify a playable programme is available.
  2. Open Public player and review the listener-facing identity.
  3. Publish or share the approved station URL and embed.
  4. Configure the station website and custom domain only after provider verification.
6. Create shows, podcasts and newsroom outputManage recorded editorial content from draft to release.

Show Builder structures programmes and episodes. Podcasts add an approved public series and RSS destination. Newsroom separates assignment, sourcing, fact-checking, production and editorial release. Each workflow keeps drafts private until the relevant approval is recorded.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create the programme, episode or newsroom assignment.
  2. Prepare scripts, sources and production audio.
  3. Complete editorial and rights review.
  4. Publish to the intended station, podcast or web destination.
  5. Verify the public result and withdraw it if approval changes.
7. Manage advertising, syndication and distributionConnect commercial and partner workflows without bypassing governance.

Promotions and Radio advertising use protected placement and proof rules. Syndication shares approved programmes or live relays through explicit agreements. Distribution connects provider adapters such as directories or CDN partners; it does not invent provider approval or credentials.

What the workflow involves

  1. Configure commercial limits and approve the audio before scheduling.
  2. Use syndication only with a recorded rights-controlled agreement.
  3. Connect distribution providers through the governed integration workspace.
  4. Review delivery and failure evidence before treating an external destination as live.
8. Monitor listeners, rights and station healthUse evidence to operate the station instead of guessing.

Listener analytics reports privacy-safe audience sessions and trends. Service insights surfaces operational changes. Rights & royalty reports build attested usage exports from recognised identifiers and verified playout evidence. Notifications identify problems that require attention.

What the workflow involves

  1. Review live sessions and listener trends in the appropriate period.
  2. Check station and source health before changing programming.
  3. Reconcile rights reports against verified usage evidence.
  4. Follow notifications and escalate unresolved incidents through Support requests.

RUVANAS HEALTH

Operate calm, privacy-aware audio for care environments

Ruvanas Health reuses the proven location, scheduling, AutoDJ, player, Studio and reporting engines while keeping each health organisation in control of its sites, channels and content.

THE RESULTSubscriber-owned health audio with rights-aware programming, minimal moderated requests and no clinical-record workflow.
1. Create sites, wards and channelsModel the places that need approved audio.

Create each hospital or care site, add only the operational areas needed for audio, then create a Health channel with an internal, restricted or public listening policy.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create the site and local timezone.
  2. Add wards, departments or wellbeing areas.
  3. Create the subscriber-owned Health channel.
  4. Choose its listening policy deliberately.
2. Build rights-cleared programmingUse only currently eligible music and owned audio.

Health AutoDJ and schedules use the shared server-side eligibility checks for plan tier, genre, territory, dates, review state and HEALTH_RADIO permission. Organisation-owned audio remains independent of catalogue tier.

What the workflow involves

  1. Review Health Radio rights permission.
  2. Choose an eligible music mode.
  3. Publish the weekly schedule.
  4. Keep an approved fallback ready.
3. Moderate requests with minimal dataA song request is not a patient record.

If enabled, Health requests collect only a song or artist and an optional display name. Staff moderation remains required; clinical details, diagnoses and patient identifiers are outside this product.

What the workflow involves

  1. Enable requests only when operationally appropriate.
  2. Tell listeners not to include private health details.
  3. Review each request before use.
  4. Remove inappropriate submissions.
4. Produce, deliver and reviewUse Studio, podcasts, players and evidence together.

Approved Studio outputs can become announcements or podcast drafts. Players and optional signage serve approved areas; analytics and reports remain operational rather than clinical.

What the workflow involves

  1. Prepare the audio in Studio.
  2. Review before publication.
  3. Enrol each player in the correct area.
  4. Check delivery evidence and service health.

RUVANAS FAITH

Connect continuous radio, live services and listen-again

Ruvanas Faith gives subscriber teams a neutral operational workspace for campuses, continuous AutoDJ, scheduled live-service handoff, announcements, sermons, teachings and podcasts.

THE RESULTA multi-campus audio service controlled by the subscriber, without platform-defined doctrine or compulsory prayer records.
1. Create campuses and channelsChoose where and how the community listens.

Create campuses and operational areas, then create a Faith channel with an internal, restricted or public audience policy. Ruvanas supplies the controls; the organisation controls its content and ministry structure.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create each campus and timezone.
  2. Add halls, foyers or community areas.
  3. Create a Faith channel.
  4. Choose its audience policy.
2. Run 24/7 AutoDJKeep rights-cleared audio ready between live events.

The shared scheduler and resolver use FAITH_RADIO rights permission, territory, dates, review state and the current plan's catalogue level. A published fallback protects continuity.

What the workflow involves

  1. Prepare eligible audio.
  2. Build the channel music mode.
  3. Publish the weekly schedule.
  4. Confirm the AutoDJ fallback.
3. Hand off to a live service and resumePlan the transition before the event begins.

Schedule the authorised live input for a defined window, verify it before handoff, and keep AutoDJ ready to resume when the service ends or the live source fails.

What the workflow involves

  1. Prepare the live source.
  2. Set the service window.
  3. Verify source health and access.
  4. Confirm automatic fallback and return.
4. Publish sermons, teachings and announcementsSeparate production, review and release.

Use Studio to prepare audio, then send approved outputs to a private podcast draft or announcement workflow. The subscriber decides whether reviewed content is internal, restricted or public.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create or import the recording.
  2. Complete production and review.
  3. Choose the correct destination and audience.
  4. Publish once and verify the result.

RUVANAS ORGANISATIONS

Run your organisation’s media without turning Ruvanas into an admin system

Ruvanas Organisations is a subscriber-operated media, broadcasting and digital communications platform for NGOs, charities, clubs, associations, community groups, civic organisations, federations and networks.

THE RESULTGoverned channels, announcements, live events, podcasts, sponsors and displays, with the subscriber responsible for its service and content.
1. Choose a template and create branchesUse familiar language while keeping one product authority.

Templates adapt labels such as branch, chapter, club location or venue. They never change entitlements and do not add CRM, membership, donor, HR, accounting, election or voter records.

What the workflow involves

  1. Choose the closest organisation template.
  2. Confirm locale and IANA timezone.
  3. Create branches or venues in Locations.
  4. For Network and Enterprise, delegate only the required location scope.
2. Create channels and continuous programmingKeep every authorised channel ready between scheduled content.

Organisation channels reuse the shared scheduler, Smart Playlists and continuous AutoDJ. Selection remains server-side and uses current review, territory, dates, plan catalogue level and ORGANISATIONS_RADIO permission.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create the subscriber-operated channel.
  2. Prepare eligible owned or catalogue audio.
  3. Build a music mode and weekly schedule.
  4. Publish and verify an AutoDJ fallback.
3. Publish governed announcementsChoose every destination explicitly.

Announcements move from draft to approval to publication. Radio, podcast, display, web-player and mobile-player surfaces are separate choices; Ruvanas never silently publishes to all of them.

What the workflow involves

  1. Write the announcement.
  2. Choose only intended surfaces and targets.
  3. Have an owner or manager approve it.
  4. Publish and review the audit evidence.
4. Operate Event Mode with fallbackPlan pre-event, live and post-event states.

Event Mode records its window, channel and authorised AutoDJ fallback. Going live is a deliberate transition. Ending the event returns control to the prepared fallback rather than leaving the channel without programming.

What the workflow involves

  1. Set the event window and timezone.
  2. Choose the organisation channel.
  3. Choose and verify an AutoDJ fallback.
  4. Mark ready, go live, then end the event cleanly.
5. Produce podcasts, campaigns and displaysReuse the shared production and delivery core.

Studio outputs can be handed to announcement, podcast or event workflows. Sponsor profiles add disclosure context to the existing campaign system. Delivery evidence confirms playout; it does not claim advertising impressions.

What the workflow involves

  1. Create and approve media in Studio.
  2. Send it to the intended Organisations workflow.
  3. Publish podcasts, campaigns or displays deliberately.
  4. Review proof-of-play and operational analytics.
6. Review rights, isolation and evidenceOperate within plan, licence and tenant boundaries.

Organisation-owned media is independent of catalogue access. Licensed catalogue selection remains plan-tier and territory controlled. Every workspace query derives the active organisation server-side and audit records avoid sensitive content.

What the workflow involves

  1. Confirm product and catalogue entitlements.
  2. Review rights permission and territory.
  3. Verify branch and team scopes.
  4. Use Product QA and beta evidence before release.
